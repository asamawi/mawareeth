## Engine implications

- Egypt needs a pre-distribution step, "obligatory bequest": detect predeceased children of the deceased, run the whole inheritance computation again as if each were alive, take his share, cap at 1/3, deduct gifts already given, remove the result from the estate, then run the ordinary inheritance on the remainder with the beneficiaries excluded. It is a two-pass computation. Beneficiaries within a group split 2:1.
- Eligibility filters for the bequest: the beneficiary must not be an heir, the nearer excludes the farther within a line, the daughter line is limited to the first generation (verify in the full text), and any bequest already made by the testator counts toward it (arts. 77-79).
- Egypt's grandfather rule (art. 22) is Shafi'i-like: the better of muqasama or taking the residue, with a 1/6 floor. Saudi (art. 212) is Hanafi: the grandfather excludes siblings.
- Radd to a spouse is a last resort in both countries and differs from the classical madhabs (Egypt art. 30(2), Saudi art. 231(2)).
- Saudi has no mushtaraka sharing (art. 227); Saudi has no akdariyya because the grandfather excludes siblings.
- Dhawu al-arham: Egypt is a qarabah-style ranking in 4 classes with 2:1 splits (art. 38); Saudi is tanzil in 3 classes with equal shares for males and females (art. 235). Two separate methods are needed.
- Haml: reserve the better of the two shares (both); Egypt adds a 365-day limit (art. 43); Saudi requires a medical report. Mafqud: reserve the share (both).
- Gharqa: Saudi no mutual inheritance among simultaneous deaths (art. 203); successive-deaths logic needs a "simultaneous or unknown order" switch.
- Killer: Saudi distinguishes accidental killing (the killer inherits; art. 200(3)); Egypt art. 5 mentions intentional killing only in what was retrieved. Apostasy was not found in either text.
- Fallback: Egypt falls back to the Hanafi arjah opinion (Law 1/2000 art. 3); Saudi falls back to Sharia "most consistent with the law's preferences", with no fixed madhab.
